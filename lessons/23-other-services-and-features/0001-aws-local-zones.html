AWS Local Zones

Lesson 01 of 1 · ~6 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · What Is AWS Local Zones? · How AWS Local Zones Work

A normal AWS Region gives you scale and resilience across multiple Availability Zones — but those AZs might still be hundreds of kilometers from your actual business premises. Even over fiber, even over Direct Connect, that physical distance adds latency. For most applications a few milliseconds doesn't matter. For latency-sensitive ones — the transcript's example is financial trading — it can matter a great deal. AWS Local Zones exist to close that gap.

A Local Zone is best understood as one extra, extremely close availability zone — an extension of a "parent" Region, physically sited near a major population center, with its own independent connection to the internet (and generally Direct Connect support too). You extend your existing VPC into it simply by creating subnets there, then launch resources into those subnets as normal.

Reading a Local Zone's name
A Local Zone's name always starts with its parent Region, followed by a city code and an identifier — for example us-west-2-lax-1a is a Local Zone in Los Angeles (lax), parented to us-west-2. A single city can have more than one Local Zone (e.g. ...-lax-1a and ...-lax-1b). Spotting the parent-Region prefix is the quick way to identify one in an exam scenario.

What Stays Local, What Stays in the Parent Region

Local Zone subnets behave like any other subnet in your VPC and have normal private connectivity back to the parent Region. But not everything about a Local Zone actually runs locally: EBS snapshots, for instance, are still stored in S3 back in the parent Region — which is exactly what lets them keep the usual multi-AZ durability snapshots rely on. Knowing which capability is genuinely local (compute, low-latency access) versus which quietly depends on the parent Region (snapshot durability) is the kind of detail exam scenarios probe.

No built-in resilience — it's one facility
A Local Zone is one physical location, conceptually like a single Availability Zone — it doesn't have the multi-AZ redundancy a Region does. If a Local Zone itself fails, whatever's running in it fails too. Also: not every AWS service or feature supports Local Zones, support is frequently opt-in, and coverage varies by geographic area — always a "check current AWS documentation" situation rather than an assumption, both for the exam and in the real world.

When to Reach for a Local Zone

Use Local Zones when an architecture's dominant requirement is the lowest possible latency to a specific metro area — closer to the same category of problem CloudFront's edge locations solve for content delivery, but for compute, storage, and database resources rather than cached content.
