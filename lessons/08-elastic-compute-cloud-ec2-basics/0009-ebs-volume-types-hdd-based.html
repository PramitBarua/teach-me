EBS Volume Types: HDD-Based

Lesson 09 of 21 · ~5 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · EBS Volume Types (AWS docs)

HDD-based EBS volumes use spinning magnetic platters and moving read/write heads instead of flash memory. Moving parts mean lower performance and no support for random-access IOPS-heavy workloads — but they're significantly cheaper than SSD, which makes them the right call for specific, throughput-oriented, high-volume use cases.

There are two HDD types in general use: st1 (Throughput Optimized HDD) and sc1 (Cold HDD). A third, legacy magnetic type exists but isn't part of current general usage.

Exam tip
Neither st1 nor sc1 can be used as a boot volume. They're also unsuitable for anything requiring random, low-latency access — if a question mentions IOPS-sensitive or random-access workloads, HDD types are the wrong answer regardless of cost.

st1 — Throughput Optimized HDD

st1 targets sequential, throughput-heavy access patterns — big data, data warehousing, log processing — where economy and sustained throughput matter more than IOPS or minimal latency. Volumes range from 125 GB to 16 TB.

Just like gp2, st1 uses a credit-bucket model — except measured in MB/s rather than IOPS, and I/O is measured in 1 MB blocks (so its "500 IOPS" maximum is really 500 MB/s):

  • Baseline: 40 MB/s per TB of volume size
  • Burst: up to 250 MB/s per TB of volume size
  • Absolute ceiling: 500 MB/s (500 "IOPS" at 1 MB per operation) per volume

sc1 — Cold HDD

sc1 is the cheapest storage EBS offers — built for data you rarely touch: archives, cold backups, anything accessed a handful of times a day at most. Sizing matches st1, 125 GB to 16 TB, and it uses the same credit-bucket model at lower rates:

  • Baseline: 12 MB/s per TB of volume size
  • Burst: up to 80 MB/s per TB of volume size
  • Absolute ceiling: 250 MB/s (250 "IOPS" at 1 MB per operation) per volume

Choosing between them is simple: if the data is genuinely cold and you can tolerate sc1's lower ceiling, take the extra savings. If you need frequent, sequential throughput, go st1. If the workload needs IOPS at all — random access, databases, anything latency-sensitive — skip HDD entirely and go back to SSD (gp2/gp3 or provisioned IOPS).
