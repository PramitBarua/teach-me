EBS Volume Types: Provisioned IOPS (SSD)

Lesson 08 of 21 · ~7 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · EBS Volume Types (AWS docs)

Provisioned IOPS SSD is EBS's high-performance tier: io1, its successor io2, and the higher-performance io2 Block Express variant. Where gp2/gp3 give you a performance level derived from (or capped near) volume size, provisioned IOPS volumes let you dial in an IOPS number independent of size — you pay for the size and the performance as two separate line items.

These volumes exist for workloads that need sub-millisecond, consistently low latency at high and predictable throughput — the kind of profile large relational databases and I/O-intensive workloads demand.

The IOPS:GB ratio is a maximum — it caps how much IOPS you can provision relative to a given volume size, not a guaranteed baseline. io1 tops out at 50 IOPS per GB, io2 at 500, and Block Express at 1000, which is exactly why the newer types are useful for small volumes that still need extreme performance.

Exam tip
64,000 IOPS on io1/io2 is 4× the maximum available on gp2/gp3 (16,000). Throughput of 1,000 MB/s on io1/io2 matches gp3's max but comfortably beats gp2's 250 MB/s ceiling. If a question says "smaller volume, needs extreme IOPS," the answer is provisioned IOPS SSD, not General Purpose — size-linked types can't get there.

Per-instance performance ceilings

A single volume's maximum isn't the whole story — there's also a ceiling on how much performance a single EC2 instance can draw from EBS in total, across all attached volumes combined. This ceiling depends on the instance type and size (only the newest, largest instances hit the top figures), and it's typically higher than what one volume alone can supply — meaning you often need several volumes working together to actually saturate it.

At the time of this course, the instructor cites these approximate per-instance maximums — treat the exact figures as illustrative rather than exam-critical, and check current values in the EBS Volume Types doc or the EC2 instance type pages, since AWS raises these ceilings as new instance generations ship:

Don't try to memorize the precise per-instance numbers — the exam cares more about the relative ordering: General Purpose SSD < Provisioned IOPS SSD (io1/io2) < io2 Block Express < instance store, which the next couple of lessons will put in context.

When to reach for provisioned IOPS

  • Workloads needing sub-millisecond, consistent latency — not just high average throughput.
  • Smaller volumes that still need very high IOPS — impossible with gp2/gp3 because their performance is capped near volume size.
  • Large, I/O-intensive relational or NoSQL databases where latency spikes are unacceptable.
