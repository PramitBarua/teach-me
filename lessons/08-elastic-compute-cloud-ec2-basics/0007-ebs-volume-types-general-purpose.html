EBS Volume Types: General Purpose (SSD)

Lesson 07 of 21 · ~10 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · EBS Volume Types (AWS docs)

General Purpose SSD is EBS's default, all-round storage family. There are two members: gp2, the original, and gp3, its simpler, cheaper successor. Understanding gp2's credit-bucket model first makes gp3 — and the other credit-based HDD types later in this module — much easier to reason about.

gp2: size-linked performance via I/O credits

A gp2 volume ranges from 1 GiB to 16 TiB. Its IOPS performance is not a flat number you choose — it's derived from the volume's size through a credit-bucket mechanism.

  • One I/O credit = one I/O operation on a 16 KB chunk of data.
  • Every volume earns a baseline refill rate of 3 credits/second for every GB of size, with an absolute floor of 100 credits/second (so volumes up to ~33.33 GB all get the 100 IOPS minimum).
  • Credits accumulate in a bucket with a capacity of 5.4 million credits. The bucket starts full when the volume is created.
  • You can burst above your baseline rate — up to 3,000 IOPS — by drawing down the bucket, as long as it has credits available.

Because the bucket starts full, a brand-new volume can sustain the full 3,000 IOPS burst rate for roughly 30 minutes before credits would run out if it weren't also refilling — in practice, often longer, since baseline credits keep streaming in the whole time. Spend credits faster than the baseline refill rate and the bucket drains; spend slower and it replenishes.

Exam tip
gp2's baseline math: 3 IOPS per GB, minimum 100 IOPS, burst ceiling 3,000 IOPS, credit bucket 5.4 million. A 100 GB volume gets a 300 IOPS baseline. Volumes ≥ 1 TB have a baseline that already meets or exceeds 3,000 IOPS, so they stop using the credit system and simply get their calculated baseline as a sustained rate. At the time of this course, the ceiling for gp2 is 16,000 IOPS, reached at volumes of roughly 5.33 TB and above — check the EBS Volume Types doc for the current maximum, since AWS periodically revises these ceilings.

gp2 is a solid default for boot volumes, low-latency interactive workloads, and dev/test environments — basically anywhere you don't have a specific reason to reach for something else.

gp3: same media, simpler and cheaper

gp3 removes the credit-bucket complexity entirely. Every gp3 volume, regardless of size, starts with a flat 3,000 IOPS and 125 MB/s of throughput included in the base price — no bucket to manage, no baseline tied to GB.

If you need more, you provision it directly and pay extra: up to 16,000 IOPS and up to 1,000 MB/s throughput. Volume sizing still runs from 1 GiB up to 16 TiB. Unlike gp2, where more size automatically buys more baseline IOPS, gp3 performance and size are independent dials — sizing up a gp3 volume does not raise its IOPS or throughput.

Exam tip
At the time of this course, gp3's base price runs roughly 20% cheaper than gp2 for equivalent baseline performance, so if your workload fits within 3,000 IOPS / 125 MB/s, gp3 is close to a strict upgrade over gp2. You can switch a volume from gp2 to gp3 (or between most EBS types) live using Elastic Volumes, without detaching it.
