AWS Organizations

Lesson 07 of 11 · ~10 min

What is AWS Organizations?

AWS Organizations lets you manage many AWS accounts centrally. One account becomes the management account (sometimes called the master account). All other accounts are member accounts.

The Hierarchy

ConceptDescription
Management AccountThe account that created the org. Handles consolidated billing. Cannot be restricted by SCPs.
Member AccountAny account that has joined or been created inside the org (one management + zero or more members).
Org RootThe top-most container. Policies at the root apply to everything (except the management account).
Organizational Unit (OU)A sub-container inside the root (or inside another OU). Used to group accounts by team, environment, or function. OUs can be nested.

Inviting vs Creating Accounts

  • Invite existing account – the management account sends an invite; the other account must accept.
  • Create new account – the management account can create a brand-new AWS account directly inside the org.

Consolidated Billing

All charges across member accounts are consolidated into the management account. This enables volume discounts and a single invoice.

Cross-account access pattern

You do not need an IAM user in every account. The recommended pattern is:

  1. Users log in to one account (often a dedicated "jump" or identity account).
  2. They assume an IAM role in whichever member account they need to work in.
  3. The role grants the access; no per-account IAM users required.
Exam tip
Remember: one and only one management account per organization; OUs can be nested; the management account is never restricted by SCPs.
