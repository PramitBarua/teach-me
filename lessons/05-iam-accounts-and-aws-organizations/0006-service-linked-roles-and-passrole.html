Service-linked Roles & PassRole

Lesson 06 of 11 · ~8 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · AWS: Service-linked roles

Service-linked Roles

A service-linked role is an IAM role that is directly linked to one specific AWS service. Its permissions are predefined by that service — you cannot change what the role can do, only whether it exists.

PropertyDetail
PermissionsFixed. Defined by the service, not by you.
Who creates itThe service itself (during first use), or you via IAM, or during the service setup wizard.
DeletionCannot be deleted while the service is actively using it.
ExampleElastic Load Balancing creates a service-linked role to manage EC2 instances on your behalf.

PassRole

Sometimes an operator needs to associate a role with an AWS service (e.g. "create an EC2 instance with this IAM role"). AWS enforces that the operator has the iam:PassRole permission for that specific role before allowing the association.

This separates two concerns:

  • Role creators — can create and edit roles
  • Role passers — can associate existing roles with services, but cannot create or modify the roles
Why this matters
Without PassRole control, a user with only ec2:RunInstances could attach a highly-privileged role to an EC2 instance — gaining access they were never intended to have. PassRole is the safeguard.
Privilege escalation risk
A user with PassRole can assign a role to a service that has more permissions than the user themselves. This is intentional — it enables delegation — but it must be granted carefully.

Retrieval Practice

1. Can you edit the permissions of a service-linked role?
