Cost Explorer, Budgets, CUR and Compute Optimizer

Lesson 5 of 23 · ~8 min

Source: Cost Explorer · AWS Budgets · Budget actions · Cost and Usage Reports · Compute Optimizer

Domain 4 of the exam, Design Cost-Optimized Architectures, expects you to choose the right cost management tool for a scenario. The four tools below overlap just enough to confuse. The way to keep them apart is the verb each one owns: explore, alert or act, export, rightsize.

ToolOwns the verbKey facts
Cost ExplorerExplore and visualizeInteractive graphs of cost and usage: 13 months of history and an 18-month forecast, refreshed at least every 24 hours. Filter and group by service, account, tag and more. Also gives Reserved Instance and Savings Plans recommendations. Free in the console; the API is charged per request.
AWS BudgetsAlert and actSet a threshold, then get alerted on actual or forecasted spend by email or SNS. Budget types: cost, usage, RI utilization and coverage, Savings Plans utilization and coverage. Data updates up to three times a day.
Cost and Usage Reports (CUR)Export raw detailThe most granular billing data: line items with resource IDs and tags, delivered to an S3 bucket at hourly, daily or monthly granularity. Query it with Athena, or load it into Redshift or Quick. The newer Data Exports feature delivers the same kind of data.
Compute OptimizerRightsizeAn opt-in service that uses CloudWatch metrics (the last 14 days by default, up to 93 days with paid enhanced metrics) and machine learning to recommend better-sized or idle-resource changes for EC2, Auto Scaling groups, EBS, Lambda, ECS on Fargate, RDS and more.

Budget Actions: Budgets That Do Something

A budget can do more than send an email. A budget action runs when a threshold is crossed, either automatically or after manual approval. It can:

  • apply an IAM policy to a user, group or role, for example to deny launching new instances;
  • apply a service control policy to an account or OU (from the management account);
  • stop specific EC2 or RDS instances in the same account.

Supporting Features Worth Recognizing

Cost allocation tags
Activate a tag (for example CostCenter) in the Billing console so you can group costs by it in Cost Explorer and the CUR.
Cost Anomaly Detection
Machine-learning monitors that alert on unusual spend. Unlike a budget, you don't set a fixed threshold.
Consolidated billing
In AWS Organizations, one bill for all member accounts. Usage is combined for volume discounts, and RI and Savings Plans discounts are shared.
Map the requirement to the tool
"Visualize spend trends" or "forecast next quarter": Cost Explorer. "Notify when spend will exceed $X" or "automatically block launches past a limit": Budgets (with actions). "Most detailed, hourly, per-resource data for custom analysis": CUR to S3, queried with Athena. "Instances are over-provisioned. Which size should they be?": Compute Optimizer.

Retrieval Practice

Answer from memory. Explanations appear after each answer.

1. Finance wants an email and an SNS notification as soon as this month's spend is forecasted to go above $20,000, before the money is actually spent. What should be configured?

2. A FinOps team needs the most detailed billing data available, at hourly granularity with resource IDs and tags, to run custom SQL queries. What's the right source?

3. Many EC2 instances look over-provisioned. The team wants data-driven recommendations for which instance types and sizes to switch to, based on actual utilization. Which service fits?

4. In a sandbox account, when spend reaches 100% of its budget, new EC2 launches must be blocked automatically with no human involved. What achieves this?
