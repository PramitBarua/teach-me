AWS IAM Identity Center

Lesson 1 of 23 · ~7 min

Source: What is IAM Identity Center? · Permission sets

In the IAM & Organizations module you built a multi-account setup and switched between accounts with role assumption. That works, but it doesn't scale to hundreds of employees across dozens of accounts. IAM Identity Center is AWS's answer: one place to manage workforce access to every account in an Organization and to business applications. It was called AWS Single Sign-On (AWS SSO) until July 2022, so older questions and blog posts still use that name.

Two Kinds of Instance

Organization instance
Enabled in the Organizations management account. This is the recommended setup, and it's the only kind that can manage access to AWS accounts.
Account instance
Bound to a single AWS account. It's for isolated deployments of supported AWS-managed applications, and it can't grant access to AWS accounts.

Where the Identities Come From

Identity Center needs one identity source, the directory that holds your users and groups:

Identity sourceWhen to pick it
Identity Center directoryThe default. Users and groups are created directly in Identity Center. Fine for small teams with no existing directory.
Active DirectoryThe company already runs AD, either AWS Managed Microsoft AD or self-managed AD connected through AD Connector. Staff sign in with their existing corporate credentials.
External IdPOkta, Microsoft Entra ID and similar providers, federated over SAML 2.0, with SCIM for automatic user provisioning.

Permission Sets: the Core Idea

A permission set is a template made of IAM policies: AWS managed, customer managed, inline, or job-function policies, plus an optional permissions boundary. You assign a permission set to a user or group for one or more accounts. Identity Center then creates and maintains an IAM role in each target account that matches the template. When users sign in, they assume that role and get temporary credentials. Long-term access keys are never involved.

SettingDefaultMaximum
Permission set session (role credentials)1 hour12 hours
AWS access portal session8 hours90 days

Users sign in once at the AWS access portal. It lists every account and role they've been assigned, and from there they can open the console or copy CLI credentials.

Identity Center vs Cognito vs IAM users
When the people are employees who need single sign-on to many AWS accounts, often with an existing corporate directory, the answer is IAM Identity Center with permission sets. When the users are customers of a web or mobile app, the answer is Cognito. Creating IAM users in every account is almost always the distractor that "violates the requirement": it means long-term credentials and no central management.
