Multi-Value Routing

Lesson 07 of 13 · ~5 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · Choosing a Routing Policy

Multi-value routing sits between simple routing and failover routing, taking a piece of each: simple routing's "many records with the same name," combined with failover routing's health checking.

How It Works

You create multiple records with the same name (e.g. three www A records), each with its own health check attached. On query, Route 53 returns up to 8 healthy records — if more than 8 healthy records exist, 8 are selected at random. The client then picks one itself to connect to, same as simple routing.

Exam tip: what actually changes
The health check integration is the whole point. Any record failing its check is excluded from what gets returned — clients never even see it as an option, unlike simple routing where an unhealthy resource's value is returned regardless.

Multi-value routing gives you active-active DNS-level availability: many resources, all capable of serving requests, each independently health-checked, with unhealthy ones automatically dropped from the response. It's not a substitute for a real load balancer — there's no connection-level logic here, just which IPs get handed back — but it's a useful, low-cost way to improve availability purely through DNS.
