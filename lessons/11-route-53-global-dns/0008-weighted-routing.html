Weighted Routing

Lesson 08 of 13 · ~5 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · Choosing a Routing Policy

Weighted routing is Route 53's simplest form of load balancing, and it's also the standard way to run a canary or A/B test — sending a controlled fraction of traffic to a new version of something.

How the Weight Calculation Works

Multiple records share a name (e.g. three www A records pointing at three EC2 instances), and each gets a record weight. Route 53 sums all the weights for that name, and returns each record with probability (its weight) / (total weight).

Example: weights of 40, 40, and 20 sum to 100 — so the first two records are each returned 40% of the time, the third 20% of the time. The weights don't need to add up to 100; the formula (record weight ÷ total weight) works regardless.

Exam tip: weight of zero
Setting a single record's weight to 0 means it's never returned — useful for temporarily pulling a record out of rotation without deleting it. But if every record for that name has weight 0, the normal rule flips: all of them are returned.

Combining with Health Checks

Weighted routing can use health checks too. If the record selected by the weight calculation turns out to be unhealthy, Route 53 skips it and repeats the selection process until a healthy record comes up. Critically, health status doesn't change the weight calculation itself — the total weight isn't recalculated to exclude unhealthy records; an unhealthy pick is just skipped and re-rolled.
