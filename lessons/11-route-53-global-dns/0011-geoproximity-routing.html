Geoproximity Routing

Lesson 11 of 13 · ~6 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · Geoproximity Routing

Geoproximity routing is the third and last of the location-based routing policies, and the one that most directly answers "give me the record closest to this user" — using actual distance, not the estimated performance latency routing uses, and not the location-matching rules geolocation uses.

Defining Resources

You define rules for each resource: if it's an AWS resource, tag it with an AWS Region; if it's external, give it explicit latitude/longitude coordinates. Route 53 already knows where the user making the request is, so it can calculate the distance from the user to each resource and route to whichever is closest.

Bias: Reshaping the Map

The feature that sets geoproximity apart is bias — a value that expands or shrinks a resource's effective service area, applied per-resource.

  • Positive bias — grows a resource's effective area, pulling in traffic from further away (including from neighboring regions that would otherwise route elsewhere).
  • Negative bias — shrinks a resource's effective area, pushing away traffic that would otherwise route to it.
Worked example
Resources exist in the UK and Australia. Without bias, a user in Saudi Arabia (~6,700 km from the UK, 10,000+ km from Australia) would be routed to the UK resource as the closer of the two. Apply a large enough positive bias to the Australian resource, and its effective service area grows large enough to pull in Saudi Arabian traffic instead — even though the UK is still physically nearer.

Bias is what makes geoproximity useful beyond a naive "nearest resource" calculation: it lets you deliberately shift load between resources — e.g. to relieve a resource that's over-capacity, or to prefer a Region for business reasons — while still keeping the underlying logic distance-based.

Latency vs. geolocation vs. geoproximity, in one line each
Latency: routes to whichever Region is estimated to perform fastest for the user. Geolocation: routes only to resources explicitly tagged as relevant to the user's location — no match, no answer. Geoproximity: routes to the physically closest resource, adjustable with bias.
