Security, Deployment and Operations

AWS Shield

Lesson 04 of 9 · ~8 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · AWS Shield Pricing · Health-based detection with Shield Advanced and Route 53

AWS Shield protects internet-connected environments against distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks. It comes in two tiers — Standard and Advanced — with a large gap in capability between them.

A Quick Recap: Three DDoS Categories

CategoryOSI layerExample
Network volumetricLayer 3Overwhelming the target with raw network data
Network protocolLayer 4SYN floods — many spoofed connections left open, exhausting connection capacity without touching CPU or memory
Application layerLayer 7Flooding a cheap-to-request, expensive-to-serve endpoint — e.g. a search feature — with legitimate-looking requests

Shield's two tiers map fairly directly onto this: Standard focuses on the lower layers, while Advanced's WAF integration extends coverage up to layer 7.

Shield Standard

Shield Standard is free for every AWS customer and applies automatically — there's nothing to configure. It protects the perimeter of the network, whether that's traffic entering a VPC in a region or traffic hitting the AWS edge network. It defends against common layer 3 and layer 4 attacks, and its protection is strongest when traffic flows through Route 53, CloudFront, or Global Accelerator. Standard offers little in the way of proactive capability or explicit configuration — it just works quietly in the background.

Shield Advanced

Shield Advanced is a commercial product. Verified against the current AWS Shield pricing page, it costs $3,000/month, billed per AWS Organization (per payer account covering every linked account) rather than per individual account — so multiple accounts needing Advanced-level protection should sit under one Organization to share a single subscription. The fee is part of a 1-year commitment (roughly $36,000/year), plus separate charges for data transferred out during covered usage.

Advanced extends coverage well beyond Standard: CloudFront, Route 53, Global Accelerator, Elastic IPs (EC2), and Application, Classic, and Network Load Balancers are all covered.

Protections are not automatic
Unlike Shield Standard, Shield Advanced protections must be explicitly enabled per resource — either directly in Shield Advanced or through an AWS Firewall Manager Shield Advanced policy. Subscribing to Advanced does not itself protect anything; a classic true/false exam trap.

The Non-Technical Benefits

Two of Shield Advanced's most important benefits aren't about technical capability at all:

  • Cost protection — reimbursement for costs incurred from attack-driven scaling (for example, an EC2 Auto Scaling event triggered by attack load), conditional on the resource type being covered and protection having actually been enabled on it beforehand.
  • Proactive engagement via the Shield Response Team (SRT) — the SRT contacts you directly when an attack appears to be affecting availability or performance, often before you've reached out yourselves. Using this requires contact details to be set up in advance.
SRT proactive engagement has a prerequisite
Proactive engagement requires health-based detection — Route 53 health checks associated with the protected resource — to be configured first. Without a health check reporting resource health, the SRT has no reliable signal to act on. This dependency (health checks before proactive engagement) is worth remembering as its own fact, separate from cost protection's own prerequisite (the resource type must be covered and protection enabled).

Other Shield Advanced Capabilities

  • WAF integration — Shield Advanced uses WAF to implement its layer 7 protections, and a Shield Advanced subscription includes the basic WAF fees needed for that integration.
  • Advanced real-time metrics and reports — available via the Shield Advanced console, API, and CloudWatch.
  • Health-based detection — beyond being an SRT prerequisite, it also reduces false positives generally, by grounding attack detection in actual resource health rather than traffic patterns alone.
  • Protection groups — group resources together and manage protection at the group level, with membership criteria defined so new matching resources are automatically included, cutting down per-resource admin overhead.
