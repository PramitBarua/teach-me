Hybrid Environments and Migration

Snowball, Snowball Edge & Snowmobile

Lesson 11 of 16 · ~8 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · What Is AWS Snowball Edge? · AWS Snowball Edge Availability Change

The Snow family — Snowball, Snowball Edge, and Snowmobile — moves large amounts of data physically in or out of AWS, for the cases where uploading over the internet (or even Direct Connect) is too slow or too impractical. These are physical devices: suitcase-sized units, or, for Snowmobile, an entire shipping container on a truck. You order one, load it (or AWS pre-loads it with your requested data), and ship it back.

Product lineup has changed since this course was recorded
The exam still tests the three-tier mental model below — Snowball (storage only) → Snowball Edge (storage + compute) → Snowmobile (single massive site) — and that reasoning is exactly what associate-level questions expect. But the real-world product catalog has moved on: the original 50 TB/80 TB storage-only Snowball device and Snowcone are retired, Snowmobile was discontinued around 2024, and as of November 2025 the entire Snow family is closed to new customers — only existing customers can still order Snowball Edge devices. AWS now points new customers toward AWS DataSync (online transfer) or the AWS Data Transfer Terminal (physical, in-person transfer) instead. Keep the architecture reasoning below for the exam, but don't expect to actually be able to order a classic Snowball or a Snowmobile today.

Snowball — storage only

Snowball is a pure storage device: 50 TB or 80 TB capacity, connected using 1 Gb or 10 Gb physical networking. All data at rest on a Snowball is encrypted with KMS. Ordering one is a physical process — you log a job, AWS ships a device, you fill it, you ship it back — so it's never instant.

The economical sweet spot for Snowball is roughly 10 TB to 10 PB of data. Below that range, an internet or Direct Connect transfer is usually simpler; well above it, a single Snowball stops being the right tool. A key architectural trick: you can order multiple devices to multiple premises at once — for example, ten Snowballs shipped to ten different offices, each ingesting data locally before being shipped back.

Snowball Edge — storage and compute

Snowball Edge adds compute capability on top of storage, plus faster networking (10 Gb over RJ45, 10/25 Gb over SFP, or 45/50/100 Gb over QSFP+, depending on what your site can use). It comes in three variants:

VariantResources
Storage Optimized80 TB storage, 24 vCPUs, 32 GiB RAM; an optional EC2-capable configuration adds 1 TB of local SSD to actually run instances
Compute Optimized100 TB storage plus 7.68 TB of PCI-attached NVMe, 52 vCPUs, 208 GiB RAM
Compute Optimized with GPUSame as Compute Optimized, plus GPU capability for scientific analysis, modeling, or other parallel workloads

Choose Snowball Edge over plain Snowball whenever a site needs to process data as it's ingested (rather than just move it), or when the larger capacity and faster networking will meaningfully shorten how long data takes to load before shipping the device back.

Snowmobile — one truck, one site

Snowmobile is, literally, a shipping container mounted on a truck, driven to your premises and plugged into data-center-grade power and networking. A single Snowmobile can move up to 100 PB of data. It was reserved for the largest, single-site migrations — think a full data center migration north of 10 PB.

Exam heuristic: single site vs. multiple sites
A Snowmobile is one physical truck — it cannot be split across sites, so it only ever makes sense for a single location with a genuinely enormous amount of data. If a scenario has multiple sites, each with a moderate amount of data, the answer is Snowball or Snowball Edge, ordered as multiple devices — never Snowmobile. If a scenario has a single site with more than roughly 10 PB, Snowmobile is the one built for that scale.

1. A retailer has 12 regional warehouses, each with roughly 40 TB of local inventory data that needs to move into AWS, and no meaningful internet uplink at any site. What's the best-fit approach?
