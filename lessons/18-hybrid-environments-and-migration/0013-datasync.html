Hybrid Environments and Migration

AWS DataSync

Lesson 13 of 16 · ~6 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · AWS DataSync Quotas

AWS DataSync is a managed data transfer service that moves data into or out of AWS, end-to-end. Before DataSync existed, this job fell to manual uploads/downloads or to physical devices like Snowball and Snowball Edge. DataSync instead automates the whole process electronically — scheduling, retrying, validating, and throttling on your behalf.

Typical use cases: migrating data into AWS, moving data in for processing and back out again, archiving into cost-effective storage, and supporting disaster recovery / business continuity plans.

This is a light exam topic — scope your effort accordingly
The course author is explicit that DataSync shows up in at most a couple of unique questions on the current exam, and those questions test whether you can pick DataSync over alternatives — not its internal configuration detail. Focus on architecture and the "when do I use this" heuristic, not implementation minutiae.

Three Architectural Components

ComponentWhat it is
TaskThe job definition — what's being synced, the schedule, any bandwidth throttle, and the two locations involved (source and destination).
AgentSoftware installed on-premises, typically as a VM on your virtualization platform (e.g. VMware). It speaks NFS or SMB to your local storage, so it can integrate with almost any SAN/NAS.
LocationEither end of a task. On-premises locations are NFS or SMB shares; AWS-side locations include S3, EFS, and FSx for Windows File Server.

The flow: the on-premises agent talks NFS/SMB to your existing storage, then communicates with a DataSync endpoint in AWS, which writes the data into the configured AWS-side location. Some AWS-to-AWS transfers are also supported directly — for example EFS to EFS, even cross-Region.

Scale and Reliability Features

DataSync is built for large scale: current AWS documentation puts the maximum throughput per agent-based task at 10 Gbps (roughly 100 TB/day), and a single task can work with up to 50 million files, objects, and directories. Beyond raw scale, it also carries these built-in features:

  • Metadata transfer — permissions and timestamps move along with the data, important for complex directory structures.
  • Built-in data validation — confirms data landing in AWS matches the source, valuable for scenarios like migrating medical records or scans where integrity matters.
  • Encryption in transit (TLS) and compression.
  • Automatic retry and recovery from transit errors.
  • Bandwidth throttling — a configurable limiter so the transfer doesn't saturate your customer internet link.
  • Incremental and scheduled transfers — not just one-off copies.

It's billed pay-as-you-use: a per-gigabyte cost for data actually moved.

Exam heuristic: DataSync vs. Snowball
Choose DataSync when a scenario needs a reliable, incremental or scheduled, bandwidth-throttled electronic transfer that integrates with EFS, FSx for Windows, or S3. Choose Snowball / Snowball Edge instead when it's a huge one-time bulk transfer with no viable network path — DataSync assumes you have a working link to throttle, not a link that doesn't exist.
