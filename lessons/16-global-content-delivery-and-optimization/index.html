Global Content Delivery & Optimization

10 lessons · ~79 min total

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · Amazon CloudFront Developer Guide · AWS Global Accelerator Developer Guide

This module covers CloudFront end to end — architecture, caching/TTL, certificates, origins, and both layers of content security (origin-side and viewer-side) — plus edge compute (Lambda@Edge and CloudFront Functions) and Global Accelerator, the network-layer counterpart to CloudFront's content-delivery layer.

# Lesson Key idea
01CloudFront ArchitectureOrigins, distributions, edge locations, regional edge caches; behaviors hold the real config
02CloudFront BehaviorsWhat's distribution-level vs. behavior-level, and why that split matters
03CloudFront TTL and InvalidationsTTL headers, min/max clamping, invalidation cost, versioned filenames as the better pattern
04AWS Certificate Manager (ACM)Regional lock-in, EC2 unsupported, CloudFront always needs us-east-1
05CloudFront and SSL/TLSViewer vs. origin protocol, no self-signed certs, SNI (free) vs. dedicated IP (paid)
06CloudFront Origin TypesS3 origins vs. custom origins, origin groups for failover
07Securing CloudFront and S3OAC (current) vs. OAI (legacy) for S3; custom headers/IP allow-listing for custom origins
08Private Distributions and BehaviorsSigned URLs (one object) vs. signed cookies (many objects); trusted key groups
09Lambda@Edge (and CloudFront Functions)Four hook points, A/B testing and migration use cases, plus the newer CloudFront Functions option
10AWS Global AcceleratorAnycast IPs, AWS backbone network, and how it differs from CloudFront
Exam scope
The highest-yield facts here: ACM certificates for CloudFront must always be in us-east-1 (Lesson 04) — this is tested relentlessly; versioned filenames beat cache invalidation for cost-effective frequent updates (Lesson 03); signed URL = one object, signed cookie = many objects (Lesson 08); and cleanly distinguishing CloudFront (HTTP/HTTPS caching) from Global Accelerator (any TCP/UDP, no caching) (Lesson 10) — one of the most commonly confused product pairs on the exam.
