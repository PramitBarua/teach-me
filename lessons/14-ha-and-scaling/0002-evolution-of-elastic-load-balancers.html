Evolution of the Elastic Load Balancer

Lesson 02 of 12 · ~4 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · What Is Elastic Load Balancing?

"ELB" (Elastic Load Balancing) refers to a family of three products, split across two generations. This orientation lesson sets up everything the rest of this module covers in depth.

GenerationTypeIntroduced
Version 1Classic Load Balancer (CLB)2009
Version 2Application Load Balancer (ALB)Layer 7 — HTTP/HTTPS/WebSocket
Network Load Balancer (NLB)Layer 4 — TCP/TLS/UDP
Gateway Load Balancer (GWLB)Layer 3/4 — third-party appliance scaling
Never pick the Classic Load Balancer
CLBs load balance HTTP/HTTPS and lower-level protocols, but they aren't true Layer 7 devices — they can't interpret HTTP content or make decisions based on it. They also support only one SSL certificate per load balancer, meaning a business with hundreds of HTTPS applications could need hundreds of CLBs. There is no scenario, exam or real-world, where a Classic Load Balancer is the right choice over a version 2 type today.

The Two Version 2 Types, at a Glance

Application Load Balancers are true Layer 7 devices — they understand HTTP, HTTPS, and WebSocket, and can route based on anything visible at the application layer. Network Load Balancers handle TCP, TLS, and UDP — anything that isn't HTTP/HTTPS, like email servers, SSH, or custom game protocols. Both support target groups and rules, letting one load balancer serve multiple applications or route differently depending on the client — something CLBs can't do at all. The next three lessons build out the full architecture and the ALB-vs-NLB decision in detail.
