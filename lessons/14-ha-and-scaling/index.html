HA & Scaling

HA & Scaling

12 lessons · ~89 min total

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · Elastic Load Balancing User Guide · Amazon EC2 Auto Scaling User Guide

This module covers how AWS architectures scale and stay available: the regional/global mental model, all three Elastic Load Balancer types and when to pick each, and Auto Scaling groups end to end — launch configuration, scaling policies, lifecycle hooks, and health checks. This is one of the densest, most scenario-tested parts of the exam.

# Lesson Key idea
01 Regional and Global AWS Architecture Global layer (Route 53, CloudFront) vs. regional tiers (web/compute/storage/database/caching/app services)
02 Evolution of the Elastic Load Balancer CLB (avoid) vs. version 2: ALB, NLB, GWLB
03 ELB Architecture, Part 1 Nodes per AZ, one DNS record for all nodes, internet-facing vs. internal, subnet sizing
04 ELB Architecture, Part 2 Multi-tier decoupling with internal load balancers; cross-zone load balancing
05 ALB vs. NLB Layer 7 rules/conditions/actions vs. Layer 4 raw speed, static IPs, and pass-through encryption
06 Launch Configurations and Launch Templates The "what" instances launch with — templates are now the only option, launch configs are deprecated
07 Auto Scaling Groups Min/desired/max, self-healing, integration with load balancer target groups
08 ASG Scaling Policies Simple vs. step vs. target tracking dynamic scaling; cooldowns
09 ASG Lifecycle Hooks Pending:Wait / Terminating:Wait states, custom actions before launch/terminate completes
10 ASG Health Checks: EC2 vs. ELB EC2 status checks vs. application-aware ELB checks; the health check grace period
11 SSL Offload & Session Stickiness Bridging vs. pass-through vs. offload; sticky session cookies and their trade-offs
12 Gateway Load Balancer Scaling third-party security appliances transparently, using Geneve encapsulation
Exam scope
The highest-yield cluster is picking ALB vs. NLB from a scenario (Lesson 05) — memorize the short list of things that force NLB (unbroken encryption, static IPs, raw performance, non-HTTP protocols, PrivateLink) and default to ALB otherwise. Close behind: the health check grace period replacement-loop trap (Lesson 10), cross-zone load balancing as the fix for uneven per-instance load (Lesson 04), and knowing that launch configurations are now deprecated in favor of launch templates (Lesson 06).
