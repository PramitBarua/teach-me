Amazon Rekognition

Lesson 05 of 11 · ~5 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · What is Amazon Rekognition? · Working with streaming video events

Amazon Rekognition is a deep learning-based image and video analysis product — deep learning being a subset of machine learning. It looks at images or videos and intelligently identifies things within them.

What It Can Identify

  • Objects and scenes — cats, dogs, everyday objects, general scene content.
  • Specific people — identifying known individuals and comparing faces across images or videos.
  • Text in images — such as license plates.
  • Activities — what people are doing in an image or video.
  • Content moderation — flagging whether content is safe or inappropriate.
  • Facial analysis — emotion and other facial attributes.
  • Pathing — tracking how people move through a video, useful for things like sports post-game analysis.
The keyword heuristic
If a scenario needs analysis of image or video content — objects, faces, emotion, text-in-image, activity, or moderation — default to Rekognition.

Pricing and Event-Driven Integration

Rekognition is pay-as-you-use: per image, or per minute of video. It's accessed via API and integrates well with event-driven architectures. A common pattern:

An image is uploaded to S3 → an S3 event fires → it invokes a Lambda function → Lambda calls Rekognition to analyze the image → the results come back → Lambda stores the metadata, along with a link to the image, in DynamoDB for downstream use.

Rekognition also supports analyzing live video by integrating with Kinesis Video Streams — for example, real-time facial recognition on security camera footage, distinguishing a property's owner from someone attempting a break-in.

Current status: streaming video narrows to existing customers
The course frames live analysis via Kinesis Video Streams as a standard, available Rekognition capability. As of this writing, AWS has announced that Rekognition's Streaming Video Analysis (and its Bulk Image Analysis feature) is no longer available to new customers as of April 30, 2026 — accounts that used the feature in the prior 12 months keep access, and no other Rekognition capability is affected. For the exam, the concept of "event-driven image analysis" and "video content analysis" generally is still core Rekognition territory; the live Kinesis Video Streams pattern specifically is the part that's now closed off to newly onboarding accounts.
