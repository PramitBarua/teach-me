Kinesis Video Streams

Lesson 18 of 22 · ~6 min

Source: Adrian Cantrill SAA-C03 · What Is Amazon Kinesis Video Streams? · Kinesis Video Streams: How It Works

The rest of the Kinesis family moves discrete records and events. Kinesis Video Streams is the odd one out — built specifically to ingest live streaming media: video from security cameras, smartphones, cars, and drones, but also non-video time-serialized data like audio, thermal, depth, or radar.

Ingest, Store, Consume — But Never Directly

Once media lands in the service, it's persisted and encrypted both in transit and at rest, fully managed. Consumers then access it frame by frame, or however granularity is needed for downstream analysis.

Exam trap: you cannot access the underlying storage
Kinesis Video Streams does not store media in its original file format on something you can browse directly — not on EBS, not on S3, not on EFS. Data is indexed and stored internally, and the only way to retrieve it is through the service's own APIs. Any exam answer suggesting you can pull the raw video off an underlying S3 bucket or EBS volume is wrong by design.

A Common Architecture: Live Facial Recognition

A frequently used example ties Kinesis Video Streams to Rekognition Video:

  1. Security cameras each stream into their own Kinesis video stream — no on-premises processing hardware required.
  2. Each stream integrates with Rekognition Video, which runs deep-learning-based facial recognition against a defined face collection of known faces.
  3. Rekognition writes its analysis — detected faces and confidence-scored matches — to a Kinesis data stream.
  4. A Lambda function is invoked on new records in that data stream, applies logic (known face vs. unrecognized face), and can trigger SNS to notify someone.

This chains four services together — Video Streams for ingestion, Rekognition for analysis, a data stream for the analysis results, and Lambda plus SNS for the reaction — into a fully event-driven video analytics pipeline with no local hardware needed.

Exam tip
If a question mentions live video streaming with analytics, or name-drops GStreamer or RTSP as the ingestion protocol, treat Kinesis Video Streams as the default answer. The course doesn't expect this to appear in deep detail on the exam — a solid architectural overview is enough.
